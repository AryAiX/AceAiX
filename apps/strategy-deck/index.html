2 · The problem

Breadth without a payer is risky with 6–10 months of runway

No one is paying

No paid workflow; athletes are free and payments aren't integrated.

Too broad for the runway

Every cluster needs QA, support, safety and roadmap time.

Social can't win

Feeds and reels compete with Instagram and TikTok, where athletes already are.

Data isn't credible yet

Talent Score reflects profile activity, not ability; Sport Fit is unvalidated.

Two-sided cold start

Athletes need active clubs; clubs need enough credible athletes.

AceAiX · Executive strategy · November 2026
The product proves the team can build. The risk is that with 6 to 10 months of runway, effort is spread across clusters that do not produce revenue.
3 · Strengths and constraints

Our access is rare; our time and evidence are limited

Strengths

  • Access to senior UAE sports and government figures
  • A working multi-role, multi-sport product
  • Youth safety and guardian consent built in
  • Events, live scoring and sponsorship already exist
  • Can combine offline events with an online record
  • Potential first-party athlete dataset

Constraints

  • 6–10 months of runway
  • No proven payer or price point
  • Product breadth stretches a small team
  • Weak performance evidence today
  • Government timelines can exceed our runway
  • Safeguarding duties for minors
Implication: use access to make things happen fast (sponsors, scouts, cohorts), not just for awareness.
AceAiX · Executive strategy · November 2026
A meeting or endorsement is not an advantage by itself. It becomes one when converted into a signed sponsor, a federation presence at an event, confirmed club scouts, or an athlete cohort.

Positions are judgement, not measurement. Cost to achieve combines the marketing, sales and product effort still needed before the bubble earns money. Talent Days: about two months to first fees, mostly event operations and marketing, little new product. Academy subscription: about six months, product mostly exists, needs field sales. AceAiX · Executive strategy · November 2026
Why two steps: raising a large seed today means selling a broad product with no payer, at a low valuation. A small bridge from strategic UAE investors (who may also be sponsors or patrons) protects runway while the first events run. Raising the seed after five events, with revenue, sponsors, assessed athletes, academy subscriptions and club trial outcomes, should support a higher valuation and a clearer story. With operating costs of AED 200k per quarter, Year 1 is modelled at about AED +238k net, with a cumulative low point of about AED −128k (USD 35k) at the end of Q3. The bridge is therefore insurance against slower events, sponsors or institutional timing rather than a funding gap, and could be sized toward the lower end.
